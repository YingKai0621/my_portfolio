### Observed MACACO shot sequence

1. **Arrival:** an extreme three-quarter helmet/upper-body crop, small mechanical movement, dark olive atmosphere and a diagonal warm graphic band. Narrow metal highlights preserve a strong black silhouette. Sparse studio marks sit away from the face. The composition holds before the camera pulls away; this is the principal lesson in scale and patience.
2. **Departure and spectral full figure:** the close-up leaves the frame; a brighter iridescent figure emerges against irregular multicolor fields. Giant horizontal type appears at different depths. The body remains large enough to read as a character, not an icon. The transition changes camera, material, background and lettering with overlapping timings.
3. **Armored detail and gesture:** the camera pushes into helmet, chest and shoulder while the hand rises. Saturated spectral reflections move over broad armor plates, while dark joints remain distinct. The opposite side retains type/negative space. Luminous rectangular forms start entering behind the gesture; they do not arrive as one simultaneous switch.
4. **Environment activation:** rectangular light surfaces and vertical architectural forms increase in scale and density. The background brightens selectively, with small graphic/pixel details and large typography. The character stays in the foreground and the camera reframes around it. A high-energy bright interval resolves into a new spatial scene; it is not a permanent full-screen flash.
5. **Platform, descent and impact:** the frame opens onto a platform and illuminated pillars. A brief luminous/ghosted figure appears above it; the mecha drops into a low crouch. The impact is followed by a held pose, then the figure begins rising. The supplied frames do not prove an uninterrupted literal takeoff pose or exact skeletal clip mapping; those details must not be claimed as verified reference facts.
6. **Introduction while character remains:** large outline studio lettering and the studio's introduction occupy the same environment as the landed robot. The figure remains a compositional anchor through the information instead of disappearing before it. This relationship motivated the unified personal introduction, not the studio's wording, branding or exact layout.

The previous H2.5 recording showed name/helmet, several large headings over the character, a short holographic interval, a sparse cyan-tower world, and a separate Bone About section. The last step broke visual continuity and repeated the arrival. Four typography windows also compressed the amount of personal story told inside the pin.

## Local original storyboard and timing

The desktop trigger starts at `top top`, ends at `+=14 × window.innerHeight`, pins the 100svh stage with spacing and uses `scrub: true`. At a 900px viewport, the **effective pinned travel is 12,600px**, versus H2.5's 8,100px (9vh); this is 1.56× longer. There is no Lenis or second scroll controller for this stage. A passive native scroll-position sample covers fast reversals; the renderer coalesces draws to one RAF. On mobile/reduced motion, content is unpinned/static. These percentages are our authored coordinates, not MACACO measurements.

1. **0–14% / Identity:** name and verified software direction are immediate; close helmet, dark gunmetal and cyan visor hold while the first rig activation anticipates the move.
2. **13–26% / Awakening:** `power_on` advances deliberately through a low-angle detail. Type enters after the camera begins moving; cyan channels activate. The spectral field begins before the next composition.
3. **24–38% / Full-body transformation:** camera opens to the whole figure; the armor becomes view-dependent spectral while graphite joints remain dark. Outline type is depth-occluded by the model.
4. **37–50% / Extreme close-up:** the camera pushes back into the helmet and chest. The material remains spectral; the character gesture develops before digital architecture appears.
5. **47–63% / Gesture and world:** real `build` clip and staggered architectural blocks/panels share the same progress but have offset entrances. Cyan, white and violet surfaces affect the reflective composition; a dark floor and future platform establish depth.
6. **62–75% / Performance and anticipation:** Explorer, Engineer and Builder text appears in one typographic composition. Camera widens, the build pose holds, and the lower body compresses before launch.
7. **75–85% / Leap and landing:** scroll-derived root arc, `descent` skeletal pose, tracking camera and fixed platform produce the authored launch–fall–crouch sequence. A small decaying camera displacement and expanding impact ring are deterministic, reversible and confined to the collision. Visual contact remains a review item because the provisional GLB contains no dedicated takeoff clip.
8. **84–100% / Personal introduction:** the same robot stays on the right of the established environment while the approved `BEHIND THE CODE.` introduction and note appear, followed by the existing Experience & Achievements pillars. The isolated Bone About is no longer mounted. At release, the dark environment scrolls directly into the dark P4 Selected Works section.

## Original implementation boundaries and review risks

- `GlacialCinematicStage` owns the only authoritative progress coordinate, pin and navigation. `cinematic-animation`, `cinematic-shot-plan`, `cinematic-materials`, `cinematic-backdrop`, `cinematic-environment` and `cinematic-type` sample that coordinate independently; `cinematic-scene` composes them into one renderer. The character URL, rig names and clip names remain isolated in `manifest.ts`/`character.ts` for eventual asset replacement.
- The field shader uses domain-warped value noise, progress-shifted cyan/violet/pink/green regions and 4×4 ordered screen-space dither. The material shader mixes a view/normal-dependent thin-film color with each original material's maps and role; black joints retain low holographic contribution. Transparent outline type is textured onto camera-attached planes behind the model and occluded by its depth.
- The architecture is original low-poly scene geometry, not a MACACO asset. The landing platform is fixed at the normalized ground plane. The root arc and camera shake are absolute functions of progress, so reversing cannot accumulate drift. Bloom is strongest only at impact, avoiding continuous flashing.
- In local clean-profile Chrome with software WebGL, representative EN frames at 0, 29, 42, 56, 75, 80, 84.5, 88, 96 and 100% showed the intended compositions with no reported JS exceptions or page-level horizontal overflow. VI mobile 390×844 and VI reduced-motion direct About were also sampled. Reduced-motion rendering exposed a Chromium compositing issue with `overflow: clip`; the static fallback now removes absolute decorations and uses ordinary visible overflow, retaining 390px document width and readable About copy. These are stills, **not** proof of real-device frame pacing, continuous motion quality, WCAG compliance or owner visual approval.
- The landing is an authored approximation using `build` and `descent`, because the four confirmed clips are `idle`, `power_on`, `build`, `descent`; there is no verified jump clip. If the owner expects the same quality as the reference's complete character performance, a correctly licensed asset with suitable launch/landing animation or owner-authorized animation work is still needed.
- The provisional character remains ignored by Git and local-development-only. Written public-use/redistribution rights are unresolved; see `docs/P7_ASSET_PROVENANCE.md`. No model, screenshots, contact sheet, personal portrait or purported evidence from the reference was added to the website.
